## What a track is

The [`/core/`](../core/) lessons teach concepts, and [`/claude/`](../claude/) and [`/codex/`](../codex/) teach tools. A **track** is what happens when you point both at one specific kind of job: an ordered path through the material, with the detours removed and the profession's real problems used as the examples.

Tracks **pull from both `/claude/` and `/codex/` as needed**. There is no "Claude track" and no "Codex track" — that would be organizing the course around vendors instead of around your work. A full-stack track leans heavily on Codex for repo-native engineering and reaches for Claude Code's subagents when a task calls for broad research; a marketing track would live almost entirely in Cowork and never open a terminal. Each step names the tool it uses and why, so you're always learning the *reason* alongside the *how*. The [decision guide](../claude-vs-codex.md) explains the reasoning those choices are built on.

## How new tracks get chosen

New tracks get added **based on real demand**, not on what seems like a good idea in a planning doc.

The [AI concierge](../README.md#-coming-soon-the-ai-concierge) on [problem-x.com](https://problem-x.com) is being built to answer exactly one question well: *given your job and the work eating your week, what should you learn?* Once it's live, the questions people actually ask it become the roadmap — if fifty people describe the same recurring problem in the same profession, that's a track, and it gets built.

Until then, demand signals come from the usual places: [issues](../CONTRIBUTING.md), discussions, and PRs. **If your profession isn't here, open an issue and describe the work you're trying to automate.** That's a vote, and it's the most direct way to influence what gets written next.
