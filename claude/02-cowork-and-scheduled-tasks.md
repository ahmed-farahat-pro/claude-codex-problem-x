## What you'll learn

Cowork is the surface that makes this whole course relevant to people who will never open a terminal — an agent workspace where you describe work in plain language, connect the tools you already use, and let the agent do it, with files and outputs in a place you can actually see. This lesson covers getting oriented in it: connecting your tools, installing skills so the agent knows how your organization does things, and running real work end to end. Then it covers **Scheduled Tasks**, which is where it becomes infrastructure rather than an assistant — a task that runs every weekday morning, or every Monday, or on the first of the month, without anyone starting it. The lesson is deliberately practical: a weekly report, an inbox triage, a recurring research digest — the boring, repeated work that quietly eats a professional week.

---

## Key takeaways

- **Cowork is the non-developer entry point**, and it's a real one — no terminal, no git, no code, and still genuine agent capability.
- **Connect your tools first.** The agent's usefulness is bounded by what it can reach; connectors are the whole ballgame. → [04-mcp-connectors.md](04-mcp-connectors.md)
- **Install skills so it knows *your* way of working** — your report format, your review criteria, your tone. Without them you get generic competence.
- **Scheduled Tasks turn a good session into a standing job.** Pick something you currently do every week by hand and start there.
- **Run it attended until it's boring.** Watch a few runs, fix what's wrong, *then* schedule it. → [core/03-scheduled-agents-concept.md](../core/03-scheduled-agents-concept.md)
- **Route output where someone already looks.** A perfect weekly digest nobody opens is a failed automation.
