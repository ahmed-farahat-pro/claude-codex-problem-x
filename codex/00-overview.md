## What you'll learn

Where Claude spreads horizontally across the places work happens, Codex goes vertical: nearly every surface is another way to point serious engineering effort at a codebase. This lesson maps the three and tells you when Codex is the better starting point. The **CLI** is the terminal agent, with approval modes and permission profiles that let you dial autonomy up or down per situation. The **desktop App** is for running and supervising several agents at once — a fleet view rather than a single session. **Web/Cloud** gives you sandboxed clones of your repo where agents work in parallel, with browser-based verification of what they built. The through-line is that Codex assumes a repository and someone who can read a diff, and then optimizes hard for that: deep repo-native work, genuine parallelism, and objectives that persist across sessions instead of dying when you close the window.

## Key takeaways

- **Codex CLI** — terminal agent with approval modes and permission profiles; the everyday surface. → [01-codex-cli.md](01-codex-cli.md)
- **Codex App** — desktop client for running and managing multiple agents at once, when one task at a time stops being enough.
- **Codex Web / Cloud** — sandboxed repo clones running in parallel, with browser verification. → [02-cloud-app-and-goals.md](02-cloud-app-and-goals.md)
- **Parallelism is the headline feature.** Several agents on isolated clones is a genuinely different way to work: try five approaches, keep the one that lands.
- **Goals persist.** The `/goal` workflow carries an objective across sessions, which is what makes multi-day work tractable.
- **It assumes a repo.** That's a focus, not a flaw — if there's no codebase, start with Claude instead. → [claude-vs-codex.md](../claude-vs-codex.md)
