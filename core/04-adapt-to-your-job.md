## What you'll learn

This is the lesson that makes the rest of the repo yours. It's a repeatable four-step framework for turning "AI agents seem important" into a specific thing that runs on Tuesday mornings and saves you three hours: **map your recurring tasks → pick a tool and surface → wire the connector → decide sync or scheduled.** You'll learn how to inventory your own week honestly (the boring, repeated, low-judgment work is the gold — not the interesting parts you enjoy), how to score candidates by frequency, tedium, and blast radius, how to let the *surface* choose the tool rather than the other way round, and how to decide whether a task should run while you watch or run without you. The framework is deliberately profession-agnostic; the [tracks](../tracks/) are what happens when you apply it to a specific job.

---

## Key takeaways

- **Step 1 — Map.** Log one real week. Mark everything recurring. Score each item on frequency × tedium × how bad a wrong answer would be. The top of that list is your first automation, and it's rarely what you'd have guessed.
- **Step 2 — Pick the tool by the surface, not the brand.** Ask where the work physically happens — terminal, repo, spreadsheet, browser, inbox, Slack — and pick the tool that reaches there. → [claude-vs-codex.md](../claude-vs-codex.md)
- **Step 3 — Wire the connector.** The agent needs to reach your systems: an existing MCP server, a custom one, or a skill that encodes the judgment. Capability and judgment are different problems; solve them separately.
- **Step 4 — Sync or scheduled.** Run it with you watching until it's boring, then put it on a schedule. Skipping the attended phase is how people end up with confident, unattended nonsense.
- **Automate the boring middle, not the whole job.** The wins are in the repetitive connective work between the parts you're actually paid for.
- **Re-run the framework quarterly.** Your week changes and the tools ship constantly; last quarter's answer isn't automatically this quarter's.
